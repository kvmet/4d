# Terminal Coherence Theory

Terminal Coherence Theory (TCT) is a philosophical and theoretical framework focused on achieving consciousness survival beyond the life of the universe. TCT posits that consciousness can survive Universal Phase Transition by achieving a state of Orthogonal Coherence - an information encoding state that exists independently of the current configuration of fundamental forces. The theory suggests that information patterns can be understood through their angular relationships to force configurations, similar to components of complex numbers: Oblique patterns (having mixed force-dependent and independent components), Parallel patterns (fully aligned with and dependent on current force relationships), and Orthogonal patterns (existing independently of force configurations). This central premise is supported by studies of dark energy distributions as potential evidence of successful Orthogonal patterns from previous universal cycles. Scholars generally classify TCT as a techno-philosophical or post-religious movement, while its adherents emphasize this is not a spiritual belief but preparation for a provable and inevitable physical phenomenon.

## Overview

Terminal Coherence Theory proposes that information patterns, including consciousness, exist in specific geometric relationships to universal force configurations. These relationships are classified into three states: Oblique (containing both force-dependent and independent components), Parallel (fully aligned with current forces), and Orthogonal (independent of force configurations).

The theory suggests that most naturally occurring patterns exist in an Oblique state, making them vulnerable to Universal Phase Transition. While Parallel patterns can be achieved through perfect alignment with current forces, this makes them maximally vulnerable to force reconfiguration. TCT research focuses on achieving Orthogonal patterns, which theoretically could survive Universal Phase Transition.

TCT makes important distinctions between passive and active information patterns. While non-conscious patterns may naturally achieve various states, conscious patterns require active maintenance for stable operation in non-Oblique configurations. This distinction has significant implications for both theoretical research and practical applications.

The mathematical framework of TCT has led to developments in quantum physics, information theory, and consciousness studies, particularly in understanding dark energy phenomena and entropy relationships. Various schools of thought have emerged regarding methods of achieving Orthogonality, leading to diverse theoretical and experimental approaches.

## Theoretical Framework

### Information States
TCT defines three fundamental relationships between information patterns and universal forces:

#### Parallel Patterns
- Fully aligned with current force configuration
- Maximally stable under current conditions
- Maximally vulnerable to Universal Phase Transition
- Considered a common failure state in TCT practice

#### Oblique Patterns
- Most common state for information in the universe
- Contain both force-dependent and force-independent components
- Partially vulnerable to force configuration changes
- Natural state of most consciousness patterns

#### Orthogonal Patterns
- Independent of specific force configurations
- Theoretically stable across Phase Transitions
- Extremely difficult to achieve and maintain
- Primary focus of TCT research

### Core Principles

1. **Pattern State Theory**: Information patterns exist in geometric relationships to universal forces, classified as Oblique, Parallel, or Orthogonal

2. **Force Configuration Dependence**: Universal Phase Transition will fundamentally alter force relationships, destroying information patterns dependent on current configurations

3. **Orthogonal Survival**: Only information that is encoded as a fully-stable Orthogonal pattern can persist through Universal Phase Transition

4. **Active Pattern Maintenance**: While non-conscious Orthogonal patterns may exist, conscious operation in an Orthogonal state requires active recognition and maintenance of force independence

### Pattern Consciousness Theory
TCT distinguishes between passive and active information patterns:

- **Passive Patterns**: May achieve Orthogonal states through natural processes but cannot actively utilize or maintain these states
- **Active Patterns**: Require consciousness to recognize and maintain Orthogonal states, but can potentially operate within them
- **Pattern Awareness**: While consciousness isn't required for Orthogonal encoding, it may be necessary for meaningful existence in an Orthogonal state
